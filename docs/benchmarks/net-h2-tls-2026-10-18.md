# Fern against H2O over HTTP/2 and TLS, 2026-10-18

The hello comparison so far (`net-connections-2026-10-17.md`) is HTTP/1.1
in the clear. This records the first measured comparison of Fern's
HTTP/1.1 over TLS 1.3, HTTP/2 by prior knowledge (h2c) and HTTP/2 over
TLS against H2O, on main at f92a3b512 (the day's HTTP/2 work, #12743, and
HTTP/1.1 work, #12630 and #12644, included), with the deterministic
per-request cost of each Fern path so a protocol's cost can be told from
the loop's.

## Method

- Box: a 4-core x86-64 container (Xeon at 2.8 GHz, 4 MB L2 per core,
  33 MB L3) shared with another agent's test runs, which held three
  compiles at 100% through most of the second pass: the one-minute load
  average ran from 2.5 to 7.5 and is given per row. Everything shares the
  four cores (the 2026-10-17 record's "shared" layout); there was no
  dedicated layout to run.
- Servers: `scripts/net-bench.d/hello.fern` under `serve.supervise` with
  a worker per core, built by `bin/fern -target x86-64-linux`; `H2=1`
  (`with_h2()`) for the HTTP/2 shapes and `TLS_CERT`/`TLS_KEY` for TLS,
  with one P-256 certificate; the server answering an HTTP/1.1 shape is
  started without `H2`. H2O 2.2.5 (Ubuntu 24.04's package) with the
  nightly's mruby hello handler and `num-threads: 4`, `min-version:
  TLSv1.3` under TLS. Both negotiate TLS 1.3 with oha; Fern chooses
  `TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256`, H2O `TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384`. H2O's response
  carries `server` and `content-type` headers the Fern hello does not
  send, so its response is about 40 bytes longer.
- Load: oha 1.16.0, closed loop, 5 s per run, `--http2` (prior knowledge
  in the clear, ALPN `h2` under TLS) with `-p` streams in flight per
  connection. Seven shapes: HTTP/1.1 in the clear at 64 connections as
  the anchor to the nightly's rows; HTTP/1.1 over TLS at 64 and 1,024;
  h2c and HTTP/2 over TLS at 64 connections with 1 and with 16 streams.
  Five rounds, each running Fern then H2O on every shape (A B A B);
  medians below, with the spread (max minus min over the median) of
  req/s. Server CPU per request is the user plus system time of the
  server's whole process group over the run, from `/proc`, over the
  requests it answered.
- Deterministic measures, one worker (`WORKERS=1`) and one oha
  connection: allocations from `/count` (`__heap_alloc_count()`) around
  10,000 and then 40,000 requests, the slope per request; instructions
  from callgrind at 4,000 and 12,000 requests, the slope; syscalls from
  `strace -f -c` at 10,000 and 30,000, the slope. What the connection
  costs to open and close sits in the intercept. The breakdown by
  function is the `-g` build at 12,000 requests, whose total agrees with
  the release build's within 0.1% (9,941 against 9,934 Ir on HTTP/1.1).
- Harness: `scripts/net-bench.d/h2tls-bench`, over the nightly's hello
  and `scripts/net-lib`; it writes every row and profile this record is
  read from.

Two load passes were run. Pass A (11:06 to 11:20 UTC, load 2.5 rising
to 6) had the hello at `serve.config()`'s `keep_alive_requests: 1000`,
so each of Fern's TLS connections closed and re-handshook every thousand
requests, which H2O never does: 31 accepts in 30,000 requests on one oha
connection under strace. The hello now leaves the count unbounded as the
servers it is compared with do, and pass B (11:24 to 11:38, load 5 to
7.5) ran after it. B's table is the comparison; A's is kept because it
ran under less load and agrees in direction on every shape. The
deterministic figures are B's; A's TLS figures were 65.0 allocations
and 29,328 Ir per request against B's 56.0 and 24,392, and the
difference, a thousandth of a handshake per request, puts one TLS 1.3
handshake (rustls' default X25519MLKEM768 key exchange, ECDSA P-256) at
about 4.9M Ir and 9,000 allocations on the server.

## Findings

1. **Fern is ahead of H2O on every shape, in both passes.** In pass B
   by 35% to 68% in req/s, with server CPU per request at 0.39 to 0.66
   of H2O's; in pass A by 16% to 84% and 0.39 to 0.77. Every run of the
   140 answered every request with 200 and no error, so there is no
   protocol fault to file. The widest gap is HTTP/2 with 16 streams per
   connection: Fern spends 4.1 us of CPU per request in the clear and
   4.8 under TLS against H2O's 10.6 and 11.7. Fern reads the sixteen
   requests in one `read` and answers them in one `sendto`, 0.19
   syscalls per request; H2O makes 1.18, its `read`, `writev` and
   `epoll_wait` per sixteen plus a `getsockname` per request from the
   mruby handler's environment. At one stream per connection the two
   loops make the same three and four syscalls per request as in the
   clear (Fern: `epoll_pwait`, `read`, `sendto`; H2O adds the
   `getsockname`), and TLS adds none on either side.

2. **TLS costs Fern about 14,500 instructions and 56 allocations per
   record pair, and that is a per-record cost, not a per-request one.**
   HTTP/1.1 over TLS runs 24,392 Ir and 56.0 allocations per request
   against 9,934 and 0.0 in the clear. HTTP/2 over TLS at one stream
   adds the same 12,500 Ir and 55 allocations over h2c; at sixteen
   streams, where the sixteen requests arrive in one record and the
   sixteen responses leave in one, it adds 1,076 Ir and 3.4 allocations
   per request. Where the 14,500 go, per request on the HTTP/1.1 path:
   - the AEAD itself about 2,600: `__fern_ghash` 884, `__fern_aes_gcm_open`
     543, `__fern_aes_gcm_seal` 473, `__fern_aes_ctr32` 341,
     `aes_gcm.Key.open` 340;
   - allocation, reference counts and copies about 3,950: `__fern_alloc`
     746, `__fern_arr_dec` 746, `__fern_bytes_range` 594, `__fern_arr_box`
     533, `__fern_bytes_into` 463, `__fern_bytes_owned` 353,
     `__fern_arr_concat` 196, `__fern_memcpy` 174, `__fern_alloc_bytes` 160;
   - the record and session state machines about 4,700:
     `handshake.Server.read` 1,029, `record.__nonce` 490,
     `handshake.__deframe` 311, `handshake.Server.write` 296,
     `handshake.__write` 271, `Protection.seal` 279, `Protection.open`
     270, `handshake.__absorb` 245, `server.Session.read` 229,
     `handshake.__server_step` 200, `server.__record` 188,
     `server.Session.write` 165, `record.deframe` 156, and dropping the
     previous `handshake.Conn` and `handshake.Server` 592.

   So about a fifth of the TLS cost is cryptography. The rest is moving
   state: a record read or written rebuilds `Session`, `Step`,
   `Protection` and the handshake's `Server` and `Conn` and drops the old
   ones; `__nonce` copies the 12-byte IV and XORs the sequence number in
   one `with` per byte (490 Ir for an 8-byte XOR); `open` allocates a
   5-byte header and copies the content out of the decrypted buffer;
   `seal` allocates the padded inner buffer, the header, the AEAD's
   output and then a framed copy of it. The 56 allocations are these.
   Kernel TLS (`with_ktls`) was not measured; it is the comparison this
   finding calls for.

3. **HTTP/2 costs 5,100 instructions and 19 allocations per request over
   HTTP/1.1 at one stream, 2,700 and 16.6 at sixteen.** h2c runs 15,060
   Ir and 19.0 allocations per request at one stream and 12,676 and 16.6
   at sixteen. The one-stream figure sits above the gate's 12,592 and
   21.0 (`TestSelfHostServeAllocsPerRequestH2`, docs/NET-P6-HTTP2-PLAN.md
   slice 4) because the client differs: oha's HEADERS block carries seven
   fields with Huffman-coded values (`accept`, a four-token
   `accept-encoding`, `user-agent`) where the Go client sends six with
   shorter ones; the path is the same. Per request: `h2.__block_done`
   1,907 (the request built from its block), `hpack.Decoding.next` 1,173,
   `hpack.Encoding.put` 704 to 737, `hpack.__name_at` 418,
   `hpack.__integer` 399, `HeaderMap.append` 401, the field checks 982
   (`http_field_value_ok` 381, `http_field_name_ok` 307, `h2.__has_upper`
   294, run on every decoded field, the static table's names included),
   `h2.Conn.read` 293, `frame.header_into` 190, `h2.__respond_now` 184,
   the two `__next_frame`s 287, and the drops of the `HeaderMap`, the
   stream and the connection 430. At sixteen streams the per-frame work
   is unchanged and the loop's per-pass cost is spread over sixteen
   requests, which is the 2,400 difference.

4. **The costs every path shares.** Per request in the clear: the
   HTTP/1.1 parse `http_parse_request_framed_from` 2,845 (29% of the
   plaintext request; HTTP/1.1 only), `__serve_burst` 545, the loop pass
   521 (`supervise_shutdown$clo0` is `__serve_loop` inlined into the
   worker's closure), `http.__shared_string` 504, `fetch.Shared.quiet`
   276 (the pass asks the fetch driver whether anything is in flight,
   serve.fern:5266), the clock 364 (`__fern_monotonic_ns` 234 and
   `__fern_vdso_clock` 130), `__serve_next` 160, `metrics.Histogram.observe`
   150. They are not this record's subject; they are listed so the TLS
   and HTTP/2 figures above can be read net of them.

5. **Latency.** Fern's p99 is 0.53 to 0.72 of H2O's on every shape in
   pass B and its p50 below H2O's on every shape but HTTP/2 over TLS at
   one stream (1,098 against 804 us; 488 against 729 in pass A), which
   is inside the spread. At 1,024 TLS connections the p99 of both
   servers is the 1,024 handshakes each 5 s run opens with: medians of
   109 ms for Fern and 153 ms for H2O, with one Fern round at 235 ms and
   one H2O round at 811 ms.

Throughput on this box moved by up to 101% between rounds of one shape
(pass B's h2c at 16 streams) as the other agent's compiles came and
went; the interleaving keeps the comparison fair within a round, and
server CPU per request, which both passes agree on to within 10% for
every Fern row, is the figure to carry. The absolute req/s are a
quarter of the 2026-10-17 record's, which ran on a 12-core devbox at
lower load.

## Left

- The TLS record layer's per-record state: about 12,000 of the 14,500
  Ir and all 56 allocations per record pair are the rebuilt `Session`,
  `Step`, `Protection`, `Server` and `Conn`, the per-byte `__nonce`, and
  the copies in `open` and `seal`, five times the AEAD's cost (finding
  2). Kernel TLS is unmeasured beside it.
- The TLS 1.3 handshake at about 4.9M Ir and 9,000 allocations, which
  is the p99 at 1,024 connections.
- HTTP/2's field checks on every decoded field (982 Ir per request) and
  the request built from its block (`__block_done`, 1,907).
- The loop's per-pass `fetch.Shared.quiet` (276), clock (364) and
  histogram (150) on every path.
- H2O's hello runs through mruby, as the nightly's does; its file
  handler would skip the per-request `getsockname` and some of its CPU.
  The comparison is kept on the nightly's configuration.
- A dedicated-core repeat on the nightly runner, where the spread was
  12% to 30% on 2026-10-17 against 33% to 101% here.
