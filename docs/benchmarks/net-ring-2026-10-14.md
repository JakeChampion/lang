# epoll against the io_uring, 2026-10-14

The first comparison for slice 5 of docs/NET-P7-IO-URING-PLAN.md. It
loads `scripts/net-bench.d/hello.fern` twice from the same binary: once
under epoll, and once with `FERN_IO_URING=1`. The server is built for
x86-64 by bin/fern, supervised with a worker per core. The load is oha
1.16.0, closed-loop, 5 s per load. It ran on the 4-core development
container, with the generator and the server sharing the cores.

So, as in net-hello-2026-09-29.md, the figures compare the two backends
on one box. They are not a capacity. The plan's fixed host does not
exist: every lane runs on a shared GitHub runner. The nightly lane now
records both backends (`net/fern-ring/*` beside `net/fern/*`). The ring
was kept on 2026-10-14; slice 5 of the plan records why.

Each figure is a median. The slice-3 ring row is the median of eight
loads at each point; the other rows are of three.

| backend | c64 rps | c64 p50 µs | c64 p99 µs | c4096 rps | c4096 p50 µs | c4096 p99 µs |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| epoll | 178,509 | 166 | 2,838 | 131,823 | 27,620 | 77,327 |
| ring, slice 1 (polls) | 178,688 | 154 | 2,954 | 122,520 | 30,449 | 66,836 |
| ring, slice 3 (provided buffers) | 180,791 | 145 | 3,130 | 124,953 | 29,423 | 67,072 |

What it shows:
- At 64 connections the backends serve the same, within 1.5%. The
  ring's median latency is 7 to 13% lower and its 99th percentile 4 to
  10% higher.
- At 4,096 connections the ring serves 5 to 7% fewer requests. Its
  median latency is 7 to 10% higher and its 99th percentile 13 to 14%
  lower.
- Slice 3 moved the ring 1 to 2% at either point, within run-to-run
  spread.

The pipelined profile uses `scripts/net-bench.d/pipeload`, which net-bench
now runs too. Each connection sends 16 GETs in one write and reads the
answers before the next batch, redialling when the server closes at its
keep-alive limit. The figures are responses per second, medians of three
5 s loads:

| backend | p64 | p4096 |
|---|--:|--:|
| epoll | 1,236,341 | 1,150,299 |
| ring, slice 1 (polls) | 1,291,128 | 1,111,336 |
| ring, slice 3 (provided buffers) | 1,202,077 | 1,126,701 |

The three are within 5% of each other at both points, which is the
spread between runs of any one of them. One of the epoll loads at 4,096
had 203 connections fail. The failure did not recur in eleven more
loads that printed their errors, so its cause was not seen.

For the async profile the server is `scripts/net-bench.d/twofetch.fern`,
one loop built by the self-host compiler. Its handler parks on two
upstream fetches per request, through the Go hello acting as a forward
proxy. The load is oha, three 5 s loads of each; the figures are median
requests per second, then median and 99th-percentile latency:

| backend | c64 | c4096 |
|---|--:|--:|
| epoll | 6,289, 9.9 ms, 16.5 ms | 4,032, 426 ms, 538 ms |
| ring, slice 3 | 6,193, 9.8 ms, 18.6 ms | 4,345, 368 ms, 573 ms |

At 4,096 parked handlers the ring serves 8% more at a 14% lower median,
and the runs spread by as much. The upstream path bounds both.

On this box none of the three profiles parts the backends by more than
the runs' spread. A hello request costs one read either way, and the
system call the ring saves is lost against a generator that shares the
four cores. The plan's 32,000-connection async point is past what one
oha process on this box holds.
