# HTTP/3 per-request cost, 2026-10-16

The first measurement of what an HTTP/3 request costs the stack, taken
for docs/NET-P8-HTTP3-PLAN.md slice 10 on main at eabc237c8, before and
after its first change. Everything here is from an Apple Silicon Mac
(`-target arm64-darwin`, one worker, loopback), so the wall-clock rows
are a ratio between two builds on one box and not a capacity; the
allocation rows are exact and the x86-64 gate pins them.

## Method

Three programs, all built by `bin/fern` from the sources in the tree:

- a server: `serve.run` under `with_tls(...).with_h3()`, answering
  `hello` to every path but `/count`, which answers
  `__heap_alloc_count()` (the shape of `e2eharness.ServeAllocsH3ServerSource`);
- a sequential client on `std/http/h3q`: one connection, 200 warm
  requests, then two counts and 2,000 GETs one at a time and a count,
  reporting the server's allocations per request with the count's own
  cost taken off, and the round trip per request;
- a load client: one connection, 32 requests kept in flight for 8 s,
  reporting responses per second.

A fourth, in memory: a client and a server `h3q.Conn` joined without
sockets on a virtual clock (the shape of `tests/stdlib/http_h3q_test.fern`),
each phase of one request bracketed by `__heap_alloc_count()` and
`monotonic_ns()` over 2,000 requests. Its x86-64 build under
`FERN_RC_TRACE=1 FERN_RC_TRACE_DEEP=1` gave the allocation sites, resolved
through `nm` and summed per request.

## Before (eabc237c8)

| measurement | value |
|---|--:|
| server allocations per request, sequential | 632 |
| client allocations per request, sequential | 497 |
| round trip per request, sequential | 48 to 50 µs |
| responses per second, 32 in flight | 34,698 |
| handshake (10 ms client poll) | 23 to 31 ms |

In memory, per request, both sides:

| phase | allocations | ns |
|---|--:|--:|
| client request + transmit | 180 | 3,331 |
| server receive (1 datagram) | 170 | 2,988 |
| server take_events | 2 | 62 |
| server respond | 62 | 1,197 |
| server transmit (1 datagram) | 143 | 2,615 |
| client receive | 204 | 3,424 |
| client take_events | 2 | 66 |
| client ACK transmit + server receive | 203 | 3,813 |
| total | 966 | 17,500 |

The largest allocation sites, per request over both sides (990 traced):

| site | allocations |
|---|--:|
| `streams.__data` through `__stream_data`: the sending half and the stream rebuilt for every stream on every packet, data or not | 86 |
| `streams.__signals`, `__opened_windows`, `__stream_windows`, `__stream_data_blocked`, `__collect`: the stream list rebuilt by append on every packet | about 60 |
| `aes_gcm.__tag` and its callers: the GHASH key, the counter blocks, the lengths block, the tag, each a fresh array, four AEAD operations per request | about 100 |
| `conn.__plan`, `__datagram_guarded`, `__seal`: the plans and payloads of each packet | about 75 |
| `conn.__opened` and `frames.Cursor.varint`: a cursor record and a result per varint read | about 60 |
| `h3.Session` on the request stream, QPACK decode, `h3q.__applied` | about 85 |
| `packet.parse` and `__slice`: the header's connection IDs copied out | about 40 |

Under the load client the server's CPU profile (`sample`, 4 s) put 833
of about 2,300 busy samples in `udp_sendto` — one datagram per response,
since the loop flushed a connection after every request it answered —
and about 900 in the stream table's scans and their allocations.

## After the first change

Idle streams left alone on a transmit, the list written back only where
a stream changed, and the loop flushing each connection once per burst
of datagrams and once after their requests are answered:

| measurement | before | after |
|---|--:|--:|
| server allocations per request, sequential | 632 | 421 |
| client allocations per request, sequential | 497 | 372 |
| round trip per request, sequential | 48 to 50 µs | 39 µs |
| responses per second, 32 in flight | 34,698 | 114,057 |

In memory, the total fell from 966 to 876 allocations per request. The
x86-64 reading the gate pins is in
`internal/testing/e2ecompiler/self_host_serve_allocs_test.go`.

## After the wire

A varint read as a scalar, a payload's frames read through one record
updated in place, and a packet header's fields read by offset rather
than through a reader record per field:

| measurement | before | after |
|---|--:|--:|
| server allocations per request, sequential | 421 | 385 |
| client allocations per request, sequential | 372 | 335 |
| responses per second, 32 in flight | 114,057 | 118,646 |

In memory, 876 to 804 allocations per request.

## After the prepared AEAD key

`aes_gcm.key` prepared once per traffic key, kept in `packet.Keys` and
the TLS `Protection`: 385 to 383 server allocations per sequential
request, 804 to 798 in memory. The GCM temporaries remain, and the
zero blocks `const` arrays were meant to share allocate on every use
(#12499).

## After the table hands itself back bare

A record returned inside a Result or a tuple is copied on the way out,
with its arrays and a retain and release of every element (#12501); a
record returned bare, its outcome in fields, is updated in place. The
stream table's fallible operations, its packet-filling chain (six stages
that each returned a tuple) and its events now hand the table back bare;
done streams are marked and the list compacted once half of it is done,
rather than rebuilt per request; the stream and request lookups scan from
the end, where the live ones are; the stream send path and the HTTP/3
session copy byte ranges through the byte builder instead of a byte at a
time; and the serve loop hands each datagram to its connection as a view
of the socket buffer instead of a copy.

| measurement | before | after |
|---|--:|--:|
| server allocations per request, sequential | 383 | 369 |
| responses per second, 32 in flight | 118,646 | 130,344 |
| in memory, both sides | 798 | 715 |

The profile under load no longer shows byte concatenation at the top; the
server is idle a third of the time, the load client being the bottleneck.
What remains, in order: the allocator, retains and releases of the stream
table where the serve loop still reaches a connection through an array
element (each datagram copies the connection record and its table on the
way in), Huffman decoding of the request's fields, the QPACK encoder's
field writes, and the stream lookup.

## After the connection's steps hand it back bare

`conn.receive`, `tick` and `transmit`, and `h3q`'s, return the
connection itself; what they produced is in its fields (`events` until
`events_taken`, the datagrams until `sent`). The serve loop steps each
connection inside the construction that stores it back into its slot,
the one shape the planner updates in place (docs/REUSE-CONTRACT.md,
known gaps).

| measurement | before | after |
|---|--:|--:|
| server allocations per request, sequential | 369 | 372 |
| responses per second, 32 in flight | 130,344 | 139,564 |
| in memory, both sides | 715 | 697 |

The count did not move but the time did: the profile's retains and
releases of the stream table fell by a third. What copies remain is
being traced on x86-64.

## After QPACK, the session and the datagram planner hand themselves back bare

Three more records came back inside a wrapper and were copied on the way
out. `qpack.Decoder.decode` returned the decoder in a `Result` and now
sets `fields`, `waiting` and `failed` on the decoder it returns.
`h3.Session.take` returned the session in a tuple and is now `taken`,
with `actions` and `events` read first. Each stage that builds a QUIC
datagram returned the connection in a tuple with the plan, and the
sealed packet came back the same way. The plan now lives on the
connection, and sealing appends to `out` directly. The TLS record's AEAD
helpers were split to keep both under the complexity limit.

| measurement | before | after |
|---|--:|--:|
| server allocations per request, sequential | 372 | 308 |
| responses per second, 32 in flight | 139,564 | 145,400 |
| in memory, both sides | 697 | 584 |
| in memory, idle tick and transmit | 14 | 2 |

The throughput row is the mean of three 8-second runs. The load client
is still the bottleneck, so it understates the server's gain. By the
x86-64 trace, what remains per request is packet receive and AEAD
open (a third), the HTTP/3 request path and QPACK decode, and the
GHASH and counter blocks the AES-GCM tag allocates.

## After the 2026-10-25 pass

Measured on x86-64 (the self-host compiler, `FERN_STRICT_IR=1`): the
allocations are `TestSelfHostServeAllocsPerRequestH3`'s reading over
100,000 requests, the instructions a callgrind slope of the server from
2,000 to 6,000 requests. The sites are `FERN_RC_TRACE=1
FERN_RC_TRACE_DEEP=1` on a `-g` build, the `a` lines of a 3,000-request
run less those of a 1,000-request run, resolved with addr2line against
the tangled sources.

The fifth row was a planner gap rather than library state: a view of
`cur.quic.buf` lent to the call that consumed `cur` kept the whole loop
state borrowed, so every connection, stream table and session below it
was copied per datagram. The fix is `docs/rc-log/2026-10-25-a-a-lent-view-holds-its-array.md`.

What remains, 103 a request:

- **QPACK, 20.** Twelve are the `Result`s of tuples the decoder returns,
  `(i64, i32)`, `(Field, i32)`, `(string, i32)` and the prefix's triple,
  each boxed: #13169's three-word return. The rest are the decoded field
  list, the Huffman output and its string, the encoder's two
  string-to-bytes copies, and the response's `date` and `content-length`
  fields.
- **The HTTP/3 request and response, 30.** In `h3q`, the `Partial` and
  its list slot, the `HttpRequest`, its body and its header map, the
  `Requested` event, the connection's stream events raised again for it,
  and the response's field list, `content-length` string and body copy.
  In `h3`, the session's events, actions and the request's frame buffers.
- **The QUIC connection, 19.** Nine read the packet's frames (the payload
  reader, the frame list, the ACK's range list, range, record and frame,
  the STREAM frame's record, frame and data copy); the rest are the ACK
  ranges recorded, the plan and its list, the datagram buffer and its
  slot in the output, the `Sent` record and its slot, and the list of
  packets an ACK covers.
- **The stream table, 15.** A new stream's send half, receive half,
  record and list slot, its halves rebuilt on its first frame and its
  reply, the reply's resend entry and the copy of its bytes.
- **Packet protection, 14.** Two header-protection masks, two nonces, the
  unprotected header, the `Header` and destination ID of the connection's
  own parse, the `Unprotected` and `Opened` records, the two AEAD outputs,
  and the `Ok` box and verdict byte in `aes_gcm` that PR #13178 removes.
- **The serve loop, 5.** The queued request, its burst slot, the touched
  connection, and the handler's start.
